## A. Editor and canvas

### T-01 · Name the protagonist and write the story as bullet points
Steps: Type "Mike" in the name field. In the text editor, type the two opening sentences, pressing Enter between them, then press Enter a few more times and delete one empty bullet.
Expected (full success): A "<name>'s fortune" field sits above the canvas. The editor is a list of bullet points, one sentence each. Enter adds a bullet and deleting a bullet line removes it.
Grade: full

### T-02 · Each sentence becomes a dot, in order, with gaps for blanks
Steps: With the two sentences from T-01, add a third sentence after one blank bullet. Look at the canvas.
Expected (full success): Each written sentence is a dot on the canvas, placed left to right in bullet order and joined by a line. The blank bullet shows as a gap in the line.
Grade: full

### T-03 · A written sentence's height shows the protagonist's fortune
Steps: Write one clearly happy sentence (e.g., "Mike wins a big jazz award.") and one clearly unhappy one (e.g., "Mike breaks his hand and cannot play."). Then edit the happy one to be unhappy.
Expected (full success): The happy sentence's dot sits clearly higher than the unhappy one's. After the edit its dot moves down.
Grade: full

## B. Sketching the fortune arc

### T-04 · Sketch the fortune arc in one stroke
Steps: With the two opening sentences in place, choose the pen and draw one stroke across the empty part of the canvas: down low, along the bottom, then up high (as in Figure 1).
Expected (full success): The stroke appears as a shaded band that follows your drawing. It covers the sentence slots it spans (x = sentence position, marked by subtle vertical lines). One stroke sets the whole arc.
Grade: full

### T-05 · Drawing speed sets how wide the band is
Steps: Draw one stroke very slowly, clear it, then draw the same shape quickly. Then draw one stroke that is slow in one part and fast in another.
Expected (full success): Slow drawing gives a thin band and fast drawing a wide one. Within one stroke the width changes gradually where the speed changes.
Grade: full

### T-06 · Erase part of the sketch to leave that part unconstrained
Steps: After T-04, choose the eraser and erase the middle of the band. Generate.
Expected (full success): The band becomes a broken line with a gap. The erased slots still get sentences, which fit the story but do not have to follow any fortune level.
Grade: full

## C. Generating from the sketch

### T-07 · The sketch generates sentences that follow the arc
Steps: With the two opening sentences and the down-then-up sketch from T-04, generate (by finishing the stroke or with the app's generate control). Read the new sentences.
Expected (full success): One new sentence appears in the editor for each sketched slot, marked apart from the written ones (blue in the paper). Read in order, Mike's luck goes bad where the band dips and good where it rises, and the story continues from the opening sentences.
Grade: full

### T-08 · The generated story is drawn back on top of the sketch
Steps: After T-07, look at the canvas. Then edit one generated sentence to reverse Mike's luck.
Expected (full success): A line with one dot per generated sentence is drawn over the band, joined to the written sentences' line. The dots come from the sentences themselves, so some fall off the sketch and the edited one moves.
Grade: full

### T-09 · Infill between a beginning and an ending
Steps: Start a new story. Write a first sentence, leave four or five blank bullets, and write a last sentence. Sketch across the gap and generate.
Expected (full success): The canvas shows a broken line between the two dots. The blanks fill with sentences that follow the sketch and lead plausibly into the written ending.
Grade: full

### T-10 · Generate Again gives a new story for the same sketch
Steps: After T-07, click "Generate Again" without changing the sketch.
Expected (full success): The generated sentences are replaced by different ones that still follow the same sketch. The sketch and written sentences stay.
Grade: full

### T-11 · Redraw only part of the sketch to regenerate only that part

### T-12 · Edit generated text, and later generations build on it
