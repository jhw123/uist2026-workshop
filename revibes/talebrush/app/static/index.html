A STORY, ONE STROKE AT A TIME

Give the story a little direction.

  1. Set the scene. Name your protagonist and write a sentence or two on the left. One row is one step in the story.
  2. Sketch what happens. Draw across empty sentence positions. Higher is better fortune; lower is worse. Release to generate. Slow strokes narrow the green envelope and consider more candidate sentences.
  3. Follow the blue line. Generated sentences are independently scored and plotted over your sketch. Hover over a sentence or point to connect them. Blank sentences appear as gaps.
  4. Try another direction. Redraw a section to replace only that section’s AI sentences. Generate Again repeats the last range. Your own words are kept as fixed anchors; clear a row to let AI replace it.
  5. Leave some space. Erase part of the green sketch, then Generate Again to let the AI choose the fortune there. To infill, write an ending after some blank rows and sketch between the anchors.
  6. Keep the possibilities. Open History to overlay past arcs, hover or focus a draft to preview it, and select to restore it. Drafts and history are saved in this browser.
About the paper and model substitutions

Based solely on Chung et al., “TaleBrush: Sketching Stories with Generative Pretrained Language Models,” CHI 2022, DOI 10.1145/3491102.3501819.

The original uses soft-prompt-tuned GPT-Neo 2.7B for recognition and generation, and modified GeDi with GPT2-medium for continuous fortune control. This reconstruction uses prompt-based generation and separate recognition calls through the workshop’s signed-in Codex bridge. It does not reproduce training or claim equivalent model quality.

Two or three candidate sentences per slot are batched into one model call, followed by independent fortune scoring. If the first candidate differs from the target by more than 0.2, the closest candidate is selected. Scores use the preceding context’s first candidates, so chosen alternatives can slightly change context. Infilling uses both fixed contexts in a single pass rather than alternating directions. Surprise is expressed as an instruction because the bridge ignores temperature. Pointer speed determines tolerance; its numeric calibration is a reconstruction choice.

Analyze fortune scores your existing text. Generation and analysis use your Codex plan. No API key is required, and failures never silently switch to canned stories.
