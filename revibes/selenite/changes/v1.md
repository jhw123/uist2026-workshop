The global sidebar (topic, options encountered so far, commonly considered
criteria), the in-situ paragraph annotations with previous/next navigation,
the zoom-in analysis with sentiment boxes, and the end-of-page summary with
search suggestions all work. The paper's Chrome extension is a web app here,
so pages are opened by address or pasted text rather than browsed in place.

## Built
- Opening a page by address, by pasted text or HTML, or from three bundled sample pages.
- Automatic topic recognition (Appendix D.1) and clustering of similar topics into one; manual rename, reassigning a page to another topic, new topic for a page.
- Commonly considered criteria: initial list plus "five more" rounds until about 20 (Appendix D.3), ranked by importance; "Request more criteria"; "Add a criterion"; edit name and description in place; pin; delete; drag to reorder.
- Options encountered so far, extracted from each page (Appendix D.2), accumulated per topic; add, rename, pin, delete, reorder.
- Page-level overview: criteria and options present on the page are highlighted with mention counts, absent ones are low-lighted; "previous/next" buttons scroll between mentions.
- In-situ annotations above each paragraph (criteria and options, lower-confidence ones faded); clicking a chip jumps to the next mention.
- "Analyze" button on hover: zoom-in with phrases boxed green/red/grey by sentiment, labelled with the criterion and the option they are about (Appendix D.4).
- Dwell-time tracking (2-second threshold) and the summary block: criteria looked at, criteria ignored, and two criteria to search for next (relevance plus diversity, greedy peeling), linked to a web search.
- Library of topics and pages so reading can be resumed.

## Assumptions
- The paper's extension read the live page in the browser; here a built-in extractor turns a fetched page into paragraphs. Pages that need JavaScript may extract poorly; the group can paste text instead.
- The paper does not say how absent criteria are ordered or whether option chips appear in the paragraph annotations; both criteria and options are shown above paragraphs, with options in orange.
- "Lower-confidence" chips (faded) stand in for the paper's descending-score ordering; the paper shows some chips lighter in Figure 3 without explaining why.
- Suggestions in the summary block are computed when the block scrolls into view and on demand with "Suggest".

## Substitutions
- GPT-4 → the LLM bridge's model (Haiku by default). The bridge ignores temperature and the paper's n=10 sampling for the topic phrase (one answer is used).
- bart-large-mnli zero-shot NLI → the bridge labels batches of paragraphs with scores; threshold 0.5 instead of 0.96 (different scale).
- SentenceBERT topic clustering → the bridge judges whether a new topic matches an existing one.
- GPT-2 perplexity relevance and SentenceBERT distances for suggestions → bridge relevance scores and TF-IDF cosine distances, with the paper's greedy peeling.
- Firebase (database and auth) → a local JSON file, no sign-in.
- The paper's dual API requests are not reproduced; its retry with a random 1 to 5 second delay (up to 5 attempts) is.
